# Industry Lens — Life Sciences: Genomics & Genetic Diagnostics

## 1. How the business makes money
- Main models: clinical testing services (paid per test), research and pharma services (biopharma partnerships, companion diagnostics), products (instruments, kits, software), and data/insight partnerships.
- Key metrics: test volume, average revenue per test, **reimbursement rate (share of tests actually paid)**, cost per test, turnaround time, gross margin, cash burn and runway (for growth-stage companies).
- Revenue is often far below "list price × volume" because of payer denials and slow collections — always ask about collected revenue, not billed revenue.

## 2. What constrains strategy
- **Reimbursement:** payer coverage decisions, billing codes and clinical-utility evidence determine whether a test is commercially viable. (verify current)
- **Regulation:** laboratory accreditation and quality regimes, and the regulatory status of lab-developed tests, differ by country and have been changing. (verify current)
- **Data privacy:** genetic data is treated as highly sensitive health data in most jurisdictions; consent, storage location and secondary use (e.g., research, AI training) are strategic constraints, not just compliance tasks.
- **Evidence cycles:** clinical validation and publication take years; strategy timelines must respect them.
- **Talent:** scarce bioinformatics, genetic-counselling and regulatory expertise.

## 3. Where value is shifting
- Sequencing costs keep falling → raw testing commoditises; value moves to interpretation, clinical workflow integration, data assets and outcomes evidence.
- Pharma partnerships (patient identification, trial matching, companion diagnostics) are a major growth path.
- AI for variant interpretation and reporting is changing cost structures and competitive moats.
- Consolidation: many sub-scale players; M&A is a frequent strategic option.

## 4. Typical first-year traps
- Chasing test volume without fixing reimbursement and collections.
- Underestimating regulatory and quality timelines for a new product or market.
- Treating the data asset as free to use — consent and governance limit it.
- Clashing with scientific/medical leadership by moving at commercial speed without clinical credibility.

## 5. Questions a strong executive asks in the first 90 days
- Which tests actually make money after collections, and which do we subsidise?
- What is our evidence and coverage roadmap for the next 24 months?
- What can we legally and ethically do with our data — and what is it worth?
- Where do we win: cost, interpretation quality, workflow integration, or partnerships?
- What regulatory or quality change would hurt us most, and how prepared are we?

## 6. Vocabulary
Turnaround time (TAT) · reimbursement / coverage · clinical utility · companion diagnostic · variant interpretation · lab-developed test (LDT) · genetic counselling · consent model · real-world data
