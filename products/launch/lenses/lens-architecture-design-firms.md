# Industry Lens — Architecture & Design Firms

## 1. How the business makes money
- Professional-services model: fees per project (fixed fee, percentage of construction cost, or time-based), delivered in phases (concept → design development → documentation → construction administration).
- Key metrics: **utilisation** (billable share of staff time), fee per project vs. hours actually spent (project profitability), backlog / pipeline, win rate, work-in-progress and **days to collect fees**, revenue per employee.
- Profit is made or lost in project delivery: scope creep and unbilled changes erode margins quietly.

## 2. What constrains strategy
- **Construction-cycle exposure:** demand follows property and infrastructure investment, which is sensitive to interest rates and public budgets. (verify current)
- **Licensing and liability:** practice licences by jurisdiction; professional-indemnity exposure shapes which projects and markets are acceptable.
- **People:** the firm's value sits in designers and partners; reputation and design culture are fragile; senior talent can leave with clients.
- **Ownership model:** partnerships and founder-led firms make decisions by consensus; succession and equity questions often sit underneath strategy debates.
- **Procurement:** much work is won through competitive bids, frameworks or design competitions with fee pressure.

## 3. Where value is shifting
- Digital design and BIM are standard; AI-assisted design, visualisation and documentation are changing how many hours a project needs — and therefore fee models.
- Growth in sustainability, retrofit/adaptive reuse and regulatory-compliance expertise.
- Clients increasingly buy outcomes and expertise (sector specialisms, advisory) rather than drawings.
- Consolidation into multi-disciplinary and global groups; mid-size firms face a scale squeeze.

## 4. Typical first-year traps
- Imposing corporate processes on a design culture — losing key talent.
- Growing revenue through low-fee bids that destroy profitability.
- Ignoring partner/ownership dynamics behind "strategy" disagreements.
- Treating AI as a tooling issue rather than a pricing and business-model issue.

## 5. Questions a strong executive asks in the first 90 days
- Which project types, sectors and clients are truly profitable after actual hours?
- How concentrated is our pipeline, and how exposed are we to the construction cycle?
- Who are the 10 people whose departure would hurt most, and why do they stay?
- How will AI change hours per project — and should we change how we price?
- Where do we want to be known as the best in the world (sector, typology, capability)?
