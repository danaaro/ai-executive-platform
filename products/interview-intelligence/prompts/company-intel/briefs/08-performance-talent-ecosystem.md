# Brief 08 — Performance, Talent & External Ecosystem
**Shelf life:** 180 days · **Output:** `research/08-performance-talent-ecosystem.md`
**Version:** 1

## Goal
Collect the public evidence on how the company rewards and holds people accountable, which
executives succeed or fail there, how much change and ambiguity it is going through, and which
external relationships decide success. See `docs/company-context-framework.md` §12–15.

## Questions to answer
### Performance & accountability (§12)
1. **Executive pay and incentives**, where disclosed (annual report, remuneration report, proxy
   statement): bonus metrics (revenue, EBITDA, cash, TSR, ESG…), short- vs long-term weighting,
   equity/LTI plans, clawbacks.
2. **Company-wide reward signals:** bonus/equity mentions on the careers page and job postings,
   stated performance-review practices.
3. **Consequences of underperformance:** publicly reported exits after missed targets, profit
   warnings followed by leadership changes (dated).

### Talent philosophy (§13)
4. **Leadership turnover:** senior arrivals and departures in the last 3 years with dates; average
   tenure of the current leadership team where computable from official bios (show the dates).
5. **Where leaders come from:** shared backgrounds of the current leadership (e.g. several from one
   previous company, consultancies, founders, same industry).
6. **Promotions from within vs hires from outside** at senior level, where announced.
7. **Publicly visible senior hires that didn't last** (left within ~18 months), with dates — facts
   only, never the reasons unless the company or reputable press stated them.

### Change & ambiguity (§14)
8. **Evidence of change:** restructurings, transformations, reorganisations, new mandates, matrix
   or reporting-line changes, resource cuts or investment pushes (dated).
9. **Political / stakeholder complexity signals:** multiple owners, cross-border structures,
   regulators, unions, works councils.

### External ecosystem (§15)
10. **Key external stakeholders:** customers (concentration, named major clients), regulators,
    government, investors, banks/lenders, partners, distributors, suppliers, media, industry bodies,
    unions, communities, key opinion leaders — only those evidenced.
11. **Which external relationships materially affect results** (stated by the company, analysts or
    reputable press).

## Sources, in priority order
1. **Official company website: the source of truth.** `company-website-*.md` in `inputs/`
   (read by the platform today) plus the site's
   live pages: leadership, careers, investors, news. When it disagrees with
   any other source about the company itself, the website wins; record the conflict.
2. **Documents we uploaded** (other files in `inputs/`)
3. Annual / remuneration reports, proxy statements, regulatory filings
4. Official press releases; reputable business and trade press
5. Careers site and job postings (public pages only)
Sources from 3 on are found by web search: use them only after 1 and 2, and never over them.

## Module-specific rules
- **People: public professional information only.** Never speculate about why someone left.
- **No demographic data** about employees or leaders.
- Most of the real performance culture and talent history is not public. Record what is, and list
  the rest under "Not found" — those become interview questions.
