# Brief 07 — Business Model & Strategy
**Shelf life:** 90 days · **Output:** `research/07-business-strategy.md`
**Version:** 1

## Goal
Understand how the company actually makes money, what drives its value, which chapter it is in,
and the real strategy for the next 24–36 months — not just the corporate presentation. See
`docs/company-context-framework.md` §2–4.

## Questions to answer
### Business model (§3)
1. **Revenue model:** how the company makes money (transactional, subscription, licensing,
   services, marketplace fees, advertising…), in its own words where possible.
2. **Customer segments:** who pays; B2B / B2C / B2B2C; enterprise / SMB / consumer.
3. **Recurring vs non-recurring revenue** (only as published).
4. **Sales cycle and distribution model:** direct sales, partners, online self-serve, agents,
   distributors (as stated or evidenced by job postings and partner pages).
5. **Product complexity and technology dependence:** what the product is, how technical it is.
6. **Regulatory intensity:** licences, regulators, compliance regimes that apply.
7. **Competitors and stated competitive advantages** — label the company's own claims "company claim".
8. **Value drivers:** what the company and credible analysts say drives its value (distribution,
   network effects, brand, data, IP, regulatory access, operational excellence…). Record who says it.

### Evolution & maturity (§2)
9. **Chapter signals:** facts that place the company on the path founding → product-market fit →
   scale-up → international expansion → professionalisation → mature growth → transformation →
   turnaround → consolidation → exit/IPO → post-merger integration. Record the facts only; do not
   pick a stage.
10. **Stated ambition for the next 2–3 years:** targets, plans, "by 2028…" statements, with source.

### Strategy (§4)
11. **Stated strategic priorities:** from the website, CEO letters, results presentations,
    interviews — quote briefly, with source and date.
12. **Announced programmes:** transformation, AI/digital, cost reduction, M&A, market entry,
    IPO readiness, integration.
13. **Stated or reported obstacles and risks:** risk-factor sections of annual reports, analyst
    concerns, management's own words.

## Sources, in priority order
1. **Official company website: the source of truth.** `company-website-*.md` in `inputs/`
   (read by the platform today) plus the site's
   live pages: about, products/solutions, investors, news. When it disagrees with
   any other source about the company itself, the website wins; record the conflict.
2. **Documents we uploaded** (other files in `inputs/`)
3. Annual reports, results presentations, investor days, regulatory filings
4. Interviews with the CEO and leadership; reputable business and trade press
5. Credible analyst coverage (labelled as analyst view)
Sources from 3 on are found by web search: use them only after 1 and 2, and never over them.

## Module-specific rules
- **Stated vs evidenced:** label every strategic claim as "company claim" unless independently
  evidenced (results, launches, deals).
- **Never estimate numbers.** Only published figures, with period, currency and source.
- Questions that public sources can't answer go under "Not found" — that's the expected result
  for much of the real (vs presented) strategy.
