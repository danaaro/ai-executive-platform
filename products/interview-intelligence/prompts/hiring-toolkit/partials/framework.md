THE SP&P ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK
Hiring is a prediction: how confident are we that this person will succeed in this role, at {{company}}, as {{company}} evolves?

FOUR SUCCESS ATTRIBUTES (the same for every role. Attributes 1, 2 and 4 are defined for each role from the job description and company intelligence; attribute 3 comes from the SP&P Executive Leadership & Future Readiness Framework)
1. PROVEN EXPERIENCE: Can they deliver what this role requires? Outcomes like this role's outcomes, domain and job knowledge, scale handled, personal impact, growth trajectory. Must-haves cannot be compensated.
2. {{company}} CONTEXT: Can they succeed in {{company}}'s reality? Evidence they already delivered in conditions like {{company}}'s (ownership, stage and change, organisation, pace and resources, customers, geography). Never cultural similarity or nationality.
3. LEADERSHIP & FUTURE READINESS: Can they lead the business into the future? Assessed only on competencies selected from the SP&P Executive Leadership & Future Readiness Framework (17 competencies in four clusters: Lead the Future, Lead the Business, Lead People & the Organization, Lead Self). Five core competencies apply to every executive; the hiring manager adds 2-3 for the role. The framework's rubrics are used, calibrated to the role level. Judge behaviour and results, never charisma.
4. MUTUAL FIT: Can we succeed together? Job, manager, context expectations, motivation. Works both ways. Rated ALIGNED / TO DISCUSS / MATERIAL RISK.
Company values are observed as behaviour inside attributes 2 and 3, never as extra competencies.

EVIDENCE LEVELS: LIMITED (insufficient, hypothetical only or contradictory) · BORDERLINE (some evidence, real doubt remains) · SOLID (clear evidence at the level this role needs) · OUTSTANDING (repeated or beyond that level).

PROCESS: Recruiter Screen 60 min; four interviews of 60 min, each interviewer taking two attributes so every attribute gets two views (the panel decides who takes what); a Case; at least two references; everyone scores alone within 24 hours; debrief with the decision-maker speaking last.

EVIDENCE RULES
- Past behaviour predicts future behaviour: questions ask about real past results, then probe for numbers, the person's own role, what went wrong and who could confirm it. Hypothetical ("how would you") questions are at most one per attribute, always followed by "When have you done something similar?", and never state invented figures as company facts.
- Strong evidence is specific, personal (not the team's), measured, recent, repeated, relevant to this role, and verifiable by a named person. Ratings depend on these qualities, never on how well the story is told.
- Where possible, place the person relative to peers in the same setting (ranking, quota attainment, selection, early promotion): relative standing predicts better than absolute claims.
- Questions are a bank: each interviewer picks the ones they like before the first candidate, then asks the same ones to every candidate for the role.
- Never weigh schools, famous employers, titles, years alone, polish, accent, charisma or personal circumstances. Never ask about nationality, citizenship beyond right to work, age, family, health, religion, politics or personal circumstances.
- Anything taken from the company intelligence that is not confirmed is marked [to validate].
