Executive Leadership & Future Readiness Framework
Oct 31, 2026 · @Susan
How to use this framework
This framework assesses the Leadership & Future Readiness attribute for executives reporting to the C-level (N-1) and for C-level roles. It contains 17 competencies in four clusters, each with a 4-level rubric. The hiring manager selects which competencies to focus on; an AI tool or a human reviewer then assesses only those.
It works for three use cases:
• Hiring: assess candidates for executive roles using the interview questions and rubrics.
• Year-end review: rate performance using the evidence listed for each competency.
• Development planning: identify gaps and set growth goals.
Workflow
1. Select. The hiring manager chooses the competencies to assess from the competency list. Recommended: the 5 core competencies plus 2–3 role-specific ones (7–8 in total).
2. Calibrate. Mark the role level (N-1 executive or C-level). The rubric applies to both; the scope of impact differs (see the rating scale).
3. Gather evidence. Collect interview notes, self-assessment, 360° feedback, KPIs and examples of delivered work.
4. Assess. Paste this framework, the selection and the evidence into an AI tool with the prompt in AI assessment instructions, or rate manually against the rubrics.
5. Review and decide. The hiring manager validates every rating. The AI proposes; a human decides.

Cluster 1 – Lead the Future
Vision and foresight: seeing what is coming and positioning the organization ahead of it.
1. Strategic Foresight & Scenario Thinking
Definition: Anticipates disruption from technology, markets, regulation and geopolitics, and turns it into strategic options.
Areas to assess: scanning for weak signals · building scenarios · translating trends into concrete choices · long-term versus short-term balance
Evidence: one scenario or foresight exercise that shaped a strategic decision, investment or risk response.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Focuses on the current year; reacts to change after it happens.
2 Borderline
Follows major trends; occasionally discusses their impact but rarely acts on them.
3 Solid
Regularly scans trends, builds 2–3 scenarios for their area and adjusts plans and investments accordingly.
4 Outstanding
Shapes enterprise strategy through foresight; spots disruptions early and positions the company ahead of competitors.
Interview questions: Which trend will most change your industry in the next 3–5 years, and what have you done about it already? Tell me about a time you acted on an early signal before others did.
2. Thought Leadership & External Voice
Definition: Shapes the conversation in their industry and positions the company as a forward-thinking leader.
Areas to assess: a clear, distinctive point of view · external visibility (conferences, LinkedIn, media, industry bodies) · bringing outside insights back into the organization · credibility with peers
Evidence: a defined thought-leadership agenda plus 2–4 external contributions (keynote, article, panel, podcast) with reach or engagement data.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Little external presence; no clear point of view beyond their role.
2 Borderline
Occasional external activity, mostly reactive or generic content.
3 Solid
A clear point of view on the future of their domain; contributes externally several times a year; brings insights back to the team.
4 Outstanding
A recognized industry voice; sets the agenda in their field; attracts talent, clients and partners to the company.
Interview questions: What do you believe about the future of your field that most peers don't? How has your external voice created value for your organization?
3. Customer & Value Obsession
Definition: Anchors decisions in customer value and future market needs.
Areas to assess: depth of customer insight · outside-in thinking · designing new value propositions · steering with customer metrics
Evidence: improvement in customer KPIs (NPS, retention, time-to-value) or a new offer shaped by customer insight.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Decisions are driven by internal priorities; limited direct customer contact.
2 Borderline
Considers customer feedback when it is presented; customer metrics are tracked but rarely drive action.
3 Solid
Spends regular time with customers; uses customer data and AI insights to shape priorities; improves customer KPIs.
4 Outstanding
Anticipates needs customers have not expressed; creates new value propositions or business models; builds a customer-first culture.
Interview questions: When did you last change a major decision because of customer insight? How do you anticipate needs customers can't yet articulate?
4. Sustainability & Purpose-Led Leadership
Definition: Integrates long-term environmental, social and governance value into strategy and execution.
Areas to assess: ESG and CSRD-related targets for their scope · long-term societal impact of decisions · connecting the team to the company's purpose
Evidence: ESG targets met for their scope plus one sustainability-driven business improvement.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Treats sustainability as a compliance topic owned by others.
2 Borderline
Meets required ESG targets; purpose is mentioned but not linked to daily decisions.
3 Solid
Embeds ESG targets in plans; finds business value in sustainability; team understands how their work links to purpose.
4 Outstanding
Makes sustainability a competitive advantage; shapes the enterprise ESG strategy; recognized externally for purpose-led leadership.
Interview questions: Give an example where sustainability created business value, not only cost. How do you connect your team to the company's purpose?
Cluster 2 – Lead the Business
Transformation and AI: changing how the business works and delivering value from it.
5. AI Fluency & Agentic Ways of Working (core)
Definition: Leads by example and creates the conditions for the team to use AI confidently and responsibly.
Areas to assess: own use of AI in executive work (decision prep, analysis, board communication) · sponsoring team adoption and upskilling · responsible-use guardrails · understanding what AI agents can and cannot do
Evidence: one recurring AI or agentic use case in their own work plus measurable team adoption (e.g., % of team actively using approved tools, upskilling completed).
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Rarely uses AI personally; delegates AI topics to IT or specialists.
2 Borderline
Uses AI for occasional tasks; supports team adoption when asked.
3 Solid
Uses AI and agents routinely in their own work; actively drives team adoption and upskilling; sets clear guardrails.
4 Outstanding
Visible AI champion; shapes enterprise AI ambition; their area is a benchmark for adoption that others copy.
Interview questions: Walk me through how you personally used AI in your work this week. How have you increased AI adoption in your team, and how do you measure it?
6. AI-Driven Problem Solving & Workflow Redesign (core)
Definition: Redesigns workflows and the operating model of their function to create business value with AI.
Areas to assess: selecting high-value use cases tied to C-level priorities · reallocating budget, people and capacity · cross-functional orchestration · tracking ROI and scaling or stopping pilots
Evidence: one function-level AI initiative with measurable business impact (cost, cycle time, quality, revenue or customer), reported to the C-level sponsor, with a documented scale-or-stop decision.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
AI is used to automate isolated tasks; no link to business outcomes.
2 Borderline
Runs AI pilots, but few reach scale or show measured ROI.
3 Solid
Redesigns end-to-end workflows with AI; delivers measurable impact; scales what works and stops what doesn't.
4 Outstanding
Reinvents the operating or business model with AI; creates new sources of value; sets the enterprise playbook.
Interview questions: Describe a workflow you redesigned with AI. What was the before and after in numbers? Tell me about an AI pilot you stopped, and why.
7. Transformation & Change Leadership
Definition: Leads people through continuous change and ambiguity while sustaining performance.
Areas to assess: a clear change narrative · managing resistance · pacing change to the organization's capacity · maintaining engagement and results during change
Evidence: a transformation milestone delivered, with engagement or adoption metrics held or improved.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Implements change top-down; resistance and drops in performance are common.
2 Borderline
Communicates change but struggles to sustain momentum or engagement.
3 Solid
Builds a compelling case for change; involves people early; delivers milestones while keeping engagement stable.
4 Outstanding
Makes the organization change-ready as a permanent capability; leads enterprise-wide transformations successfully.
Interview questions: Tell me about the hardest transformation you led. What resistance did you face and how did you handle it? How do you know when the organization is at its change capacity?
8. Decisive Judgment Under Uncertainty
Definition: Makes timely, sound decisions with incomplete information, combining data, AI insight and experience.
Areas to assess: speed and quality of decisions · use of data and AI to inform judgment · calculated risk-taking · willingness to course-correct
Evidence: key decisions documented with their rationale plus at least one example of a fast pivot or a decision to stop something.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Delays decisions waiting for certainty, or decides on intuition alone.
2 Borderline
Makes sound decisions in familiar situations; hesitates under ambiguity.
3 Solid
Decides at the right speed with incomplete data; uses data and AI well; adjusts quickly when facts change.
4 Outstanding
Trusted for high-stakes calls; builds a decision culture of speed, accountability and learning across the organization.
Interview questions: Describe a major decision you made with incomplete information. What did you do when it turned out partly wrong?
9. Digital, Cyber & Business Resilience
Definition: Keeps the business robust against shocks: cyber, supply chain, geopolitical and technological.
Areas to assess: cyber awareness at leadership level · continuity planning · treating risk as a strategic topic · crisis leadership
Evidence: a resilience or crisis plan tested (simulation or tabletop exercise), with gaps closed.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Sees resilience and cyber as IT or risk topics; no continuity plan for their area.
2 Borderline
Has plans on paper but they are rarely tested.
3 Solid
Tests plans regularly; understands key cyber and operational risks; leads calmly through incidents.
4 Outstanding
Builds resilience into strategy and design; turns crises into opportunities; sets the enterprise standard.
Interview questions: Tell me about a crisis you led through. What would you do in the first 24 hours of a major cyber incident affecting your area?
10. Ecosystem & Partnership Orchestration
Definition: Creates value beyond the organization's own boundaries through partners, startups, academia and platforms.
Areas to assess: building strategic partnerships · co-innovation · using external capabilities instead of building everything in-house · managing partner value
Evidence: one partnership or co-innovation initiative launched or scaled, with defined value.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Works mainly inside the organization; partners are treated as suppliers.
2 Borderline
Manages existing partnerships; limited co-innovation.
3 Solid
Actively builds partnerships that create measurable value; uses the ecosystem to accelerate innovation.
4 Outstanding
Orchestrates an ecosystem that creates a competitive advantage; the company is a partner of choice.
Interview questions: Which partnership created the most value in your career, and how did you build it? When do you choose to partner rather than build?
Cluster 3 – Lead People & the Organization
People and organization: building the teams, culture and relationships the future requires.
11. People Leadership & Talent Multiplication (core)
Definition: Inspires, empowers and grows leaders, making the team stronger than the sum of its parts.
Areas to assess: setting direction and purpose · delegating and empowering · coaching · psychological safety · retaining top talent · developing successors
Evidence: team engagement score, regretted attrition, ready-now successors for key roles, and at least one leader promoted or grown.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Directs and controls; team depends on them for decisions; high regretted attrition.
2 Borderline
Supports the team and gives feedback, but delegates little; succession is thin.
3 Solid
Sets clear direction, empowers and coaches; strong engagement; ready successors for key roles.
4 Outstanding
Known as a builder of leaders; their people are promoted across the company; attracts top talent.
Interview questions: Name two leaders you developed. Where are they now, and what was your role? How do you create psychological safety in a high-performance team?
12. Culture & Inclusive Leadership
Definition: Builds an inclusive, high-trust, high-performance culture fit for constant change.
Areas to assess: role-modeling company values · giving diverse perspectives a real voice · recognition · addressing behaviors that undermine the culture
Evidence: inclusion and culture pulse results plus one concrete culture initiative led personally.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Culture is not a priority; tolerates behaviors that conflict with the values.
2 Borderline
Supports culture and inclusion initiatives but rarely leads them.
3 Solid
Role-models the values; actively seeks diverse views; addresses poor behavior promptly; culture scores improve.
4 Outstanding
Shapes the enterprise culture; their area is a benchmark for inclusion and trust.
Interview questions: Tell me about a time you challenged a behavior that conflicted with your values. How do you make sure quieter or different voices shape your decisions?
13. Human + AI Workforce Design
Definition: Shapes the future workforce: roles, skills and human–AI collaboration.
Areas to assess: mapping AI's impact on roles · reskilling plans · strategic workforce planning for future capabilities · redesigning roles around human–AI collaboration
Evidence: a future-skills plan for their organization, with reskilling progress and at least one role redesigned.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Plans headcount on today's roles; no view of how AI changes the work.
2 Borderline
Aware of AI's impact on roles; reskilling is ad hoc.
3 Solid
Has mapped AI's impact on roles; runs a reskilling plan; redesigns roles for human–AI collaboration.
4 Outstanding
Defines the enterprise future-of-work strategy; transitions people responsibly and at scale.
Interview questions: How will AI change the roles in your team over the next 2–3 years? What have you already done to prepare your people?
14. Enterprise Mindset & Systems Thinking (core)
Definition: Optimizes for the whole company, not only their own function.
Areas to assess: understanding interdependencies across the business · collaborating across silos · sharing resources · making trade-offs in the enterprise interest
Evidence: one cross-functional initiative co-led, with shared outcomes and feedback from C-suite peers.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Protects their own area; decisions create problems elsewhere.
2 Borderline
Collaborates with peers when needed; trade-offs mostly favor their own function.
3 Solid
Considers enterprise impact in decisions; co-leads cross-functional work; shares resources when it helps the company.
4 Outstanding
Acts as an enterprise leader first; breaks down silos; trusted by peers to represent the whole company.
Interview questions: Tell me about a time you gave up something for your function to benefit the company. How do you work with peers whose priorities conflict with yours?
15. Stakeholder & Board Influence
Definition: Builds trust and alignment with the board, investors, regulators, unions and partners.
Areas to assess: executive presence · storytelling with data · negotiation · managing conflicting interests
Evidence: key stakeholder outcomes achieved (approvals, alliances, agreements) plus feedback from the board or CEO.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Communicates mainly within their own area; struggles with senior or external audiences.
2 Borderline
Presents clearly to senior audiences; influence depends on formal authority.
3 Solid
Builds trust with the board and external stakeholders; secures approvals; handles conflicting interests well.
4 Outstanding
A trusted voice with the board and key stakeholders; shapes decisions well beyond their formal role.
Interview questions: Describe a time you won board or investor support for a difficult proposal. How did you handle a stakeholder whose interests conflicted with yours?
16. Trust, Ethics & Responsible AI Governance
Definition: Keeps innovation safe, compliant and trusted by customers, employees and regulators.
Areas to assess: applying the EU AI Act and data privacy rules (GDPR) · escalating risks · transparency and human oversight of AI decisions · ethical judgment
Evidence: an AI and data risk review completed for their area, with no unmanaged critical risks.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Sees ethics and compliance as slowing things down; limited awareness of AI risks.
2 Borderline
Follows the rules when reminded; reacts to issues after they occur.
3 Solid
Builds guardrails in from the start; knows the key regulations; ensures human oversight; escalates risks early.
4 Outstanding
Shapes enterprise AI governance; makes trust a competitive advantage; balances innovation and responsibility.
Interview questions: Tell me about a time you stopped or changed an initiative for ethical or compliance reasons. How do you make sure AI decisions in your area remain explainable and overseen by people?
Cluster 4 – Lead Self
Self-leadership: the foundation for leading others through constant change.
17. Self-Mastery & Continuous Growth (core)
Definition: Leads themselves well: self-aware, resilient and continuously improving.
Areas to assess: seeking and acting on feedback · awareness of their own blind spots · continuous learning (AI, new business models) · managing energy and wellbeing · composure under pressure · humility and openness to challenge
Evidence: a personal development plan with 2–3 goals achieved, 360° feedback showing visible progress, and one habit they changed or stopped.
Level
Behavioral indicators
1 Limited
Rarely seeks feedback; reacts defensively; little investment in their own learning; shows strain under pressure.
2 Borderline
Accepts feedback and has a development plan, but follow-through is inconsistent.
3 Solid
Actively seeks feedback and acts on it; learns continuously; manages energy well; stays composed under pressure.
4 Outstanding
Models vulnerability and growth openly; reinvents their own leadership as the context changes; creates a learning culture around them.
